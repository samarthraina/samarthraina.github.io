Mechanistic Interpretability  ·  LLM Behavior  ·  Multi-Agent Safety

My work focuses on making increasingly capable AI systems safer by understanding their behavior, the internal computations behind it, and the dynamics that arise when they interact.

The aim is to turn that understanding into better ways of aligning, intervening on, and overseeing these systems.

Where the work begins

What does it take for an AI system to remain safe as it becomes more capable?

My route into this question starts with what a model does, moves toward what is happening internally, and then expands to what changes when several models interact.

Behavior

Start with what the system actually does

Alignment first appears through behavior: whether a model follows the intended objective, remains reliable under changing context, and avoids unsafe or deceptive actions.

Mechanism

Then ask what produced that behavior

Outputs can reveal that something changed, but not why. Mechanistic interpretability lets us connect observable behavior to internal representations and computation, then test those explanations through intervention.

Interaction

Expand from individual models to interacting systems

As AI systems begin coordinating, competing, and influencing one another, safety may depend on more than the alignment of each model in isolation. The interaction itself becomes part of the alignment problem.

Intervention

Use understanding to improve safety

The longer-term goal is not only to identify unsafe dynamics, but to develop interventions and oversight methods grounded in evidence about how the systems actually work.
