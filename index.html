About

An AI alignment and safety researcher who would rather look inside a model than take its behavior at face value. The work uses mechanistic interpretability, both established techniques and new diagnostics, to study what actually changes within a model as it acts honestly or slips into deceptive and unsafe behavior.

It sits where machine learning and deep learning meet the cognitive and behavioral sciences, because honesty, deception, and how values take hold are questions as old in humans as they are new in machines. The aim is to make a model's inner alignment legible and measurable, and to help build systems that are genuinely honest, helpful, and harmless, even as they begin to reason and collaborate, with one another and with us.

Currently leading the day-to-day research of a 10+ member AI alignment group at BITS Pilani, under Prof. Amitava Das and mentors from Google DeepMind, Google, and Amazon.

Research

Papers currently under review.

D-STEER: Preference Alignment Techniques Learn to Behave, Not to Believe

Does preference alignment (DPO) genuinely change a model's internal representations, or mainly install a compact steering effect on its outputs? The evidence points to a recoverable, low-rank, late-layer mechanism, suggesting this form of alignment is often behavioral rather than deeply internalized.

Read the preprint →

MENTIS: What Belief Changes Under Alignment? Measuring Multi-Scale Latent Torsion in Language Models

A geometry-first framework for measuring how alignment reorganizes a model's internals. It shows alignment-induced change is selective rather than uniform and concentrated at particular depths, separating alignment-specific structure from the effects of generic fine-tuning.

Read the preprint →

Silent Social Inference in Large Language Models

Language models quietly absorb social context, implied identity, competitive stakes, and reputation, into their judgments while rarely acknowledging it. A large, controlled study characterizes the effect and locates where this integration happens inside the model, with direct implications for using models as evaluators.

Refusal in the Trajectory: θ-Guided Safety Steering

Using the per-layer geometry of a model's hidden-state trajectory, this work locates where safety behavior is represented, and shows that targeted steering at those layers can substantially strengthen or suppress refusal, evidence that the identified direction is genuinely causal.

nDNA: The Semantic Helix of Large Language Models

A coordinate-invariant characterization of a model's internal computation that reads operations such as fine-tuning, merging, distillation, and alignment as measurable geometric change, a fingerprint for monitoring a model's internal state.

Subspace-Aware Quantisation: A Mechanistic Study of How Low-Bit Quantisation Distorts Internal Representations in Transformer Language Models

A mechanistic account of how low-bit quantisation distorts a model's internal features, identifying which directions are most damaged and most important, and proposing a subspace-aware scheme that protects the ones that matter.
