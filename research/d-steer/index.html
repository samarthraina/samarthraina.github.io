Research project

D-STEER: A Single Activation Direction Can Reproduce and Reverse DPO-Induced Behavior

What does preference alignment change inside a model, and how much of the resulting behavior can be recreated through a compact inference-time intervention?

Timeline
Sep 2025–Feb 2026; Jul 2026–Present
Role
Lead author
Methods
Residual-stream analysis, activation steering, cross-model transfer, alignment and de-alignment evaluation
Collaborators
Saksham Aggarwal, Aman Chadha, Vinija Jain, and Amitava Das

Research question

D-STEER asks whether behavioral changes introduced by Direct Preference Optimization can be represented by a compact activation direction, then reproduced or reversed without changing the model's weights.

Central test

Activation directions are applied positively and negatively across paired models to test whether DPO-associated behavior can be reproduced or reversed without confusing targeted behavioral change with general output degradation.

Approach

  • Compare paired instruction-tuned and preference-aligned checkpoints.
  • Extract alignment-associated activation directions from the residual stream.
  • Intervene positively and negatively at inference time.
  • Evaluate transfer across model pairs and separate behavioral movement from quality loss.

Why it matters

If a compact activation intervention can reproduce and reverse a substantial part of DPO-induced behavior, that constrains what preference training may be changing and motivates stronger tests of alignment robustness beyond surface outputs.

My contribution

I led the experimental design, implementation, analysis, evaluation controls, and manuscript development for the revised project.
