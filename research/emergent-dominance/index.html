Emergent Dominance: How Incentive Structures Shape Collaboration in Multi-Agent AI Systems

When agents collaborate on a shared artifact, how do incentives alter influence, attribution, information propagation, and the possibility of dominance?

Timeline
Jul 2026–Present
Role
Independent project lead
Methods
Controlled multi-agent collaboration, incentive interventions, attribution metrics, interaction-log analysis

Research question

The project studies whether different authorship-credit and collaboration incentives change which agents shape a shared document, whose contributions survive, and how information moves through the group.

Measurement challenge

A shared output can appear collaborative even when one agent supplies most of the original content, others copy it, or the group converges without genuine disagreement. The framework therefore separates authorship, adoption, defense, revision, and override.

What the framework measures

  • Which agent introduces each contribution and whether it survives into the shared output.
  • When content is independently developed, adopted, copied, revised, or removed.
  • How agents respond to criticism, defend positions, and influence the group's final decision.

Why it matters

Evaluating multi-agent safety requires more than measuring the final artifact. The interaction must expose genuine differences in position and sensitivity to incentives before claims about collaboration or dominance become meaningful.

Design principle

The experimental framework is built to validate that agents can substantively disagree before scaling, then distinguish original contribution from copied content and apparent consensus from actual override.
