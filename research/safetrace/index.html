Research project

SafeTrace: Tracking Safety-Relevant Representations Across Transformer Layers

How do safe and unsafe prompts propagate through an aligned model, where do their trajectories diverge, and can those differences support causal intervention?

Timeline
Mar–Jul 2026
Role
Lead author
Methods
Layerwise trajectory geometry, controlled model comparisons, activation patching, and steering
Collaborators
Amit Dhanda, Vinija Jain, and Aman Chadha

Research question

SafeTrace studies the layer-by-layer propagation of safety-relevant representations in aligned and less-aligned models. The goal is to localize where the processing trajectories separate, then test whether the identified directions contribute to behavior.

Central test

The project localizes layerwise divergence between safe and unsafe processing, then uses targeted positive and negative steering to test whether the identified directions causally influence refusal behavior.

Approach

  • Measure layerwise trajectory differences for safe and unsafe prompts.
  • Compare instruction-tuned and preference-aligned checkpoints with content-matched controls.
  • Localize the strongest directional divergence.
  • Use positive and negative steering to test causal directionality.

Why it matters

Behavioral refusal rates reveal what a model does, but not how safety-relevant processing unfolds across depth. A trajectory-level view can expose where intervention is most effective and where apparent safety remains fragile.

My contribution

I led the trajectory analysis, controlled comparisons, mechanistic localization, steering experiments, interpretation, and manuscript development.
