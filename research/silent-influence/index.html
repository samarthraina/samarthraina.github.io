Research project

Silent Influence: How Minimal Inter-Agent Context Shapes LLM Behavior

Can a small amount of information from an earlier agent interaction causally change how an LLM behaves toward another agent, even when that influence is absent from the model's own explanation?

Timeline
Mar 2026–Present
Role
Lead author and project lead
Methods
Paired interventions, explanation analysis, internal readouts, causal tracing, ablation, and steering
Collaborators
Vidhi Bhutani, Atharva Singh Rathore, Aman Chadha, Vinija Jain, and Amitava Das

Research question

The project asks whether minimal context from a previous interaction can silently influence a model's later judgment. The central intervention holds the task, response, rubric, and model fixed while varying only the earlier agent's reported score.

Central test

The study tests whether unfavorable prior-score context can shift observable judgments without being acknowledged in a model's explanation, and whether internal analysis can identify the computation associated with that shift.

Approach

  • Large-scale, prompt-level paired behavioral interventions.
  • Analysis of whether explanations acknowledge the manipulated context.
  • Layerwise internal readouts and causal localization.
  • Representation ablation and steering to test whether identified directions contribute causally.

Why it matters

AI systems increasingly evaluate, supervise, and communicate with other models. If small pieces of inter-agent context can alter judgment without appearing in the model's explanation, output-only monitoring may miss an important source of bias and instability.

My contribution

I led the reanalysis of the original behavioral study, identified prior-score context as the strongest phenomenon, reframed the research around minimal inter-agent context and silent influence, designed the expanded behavioral and mechanistic experiments, and coordinate the experimental and manuscript work.
